Our Accomplishments

Here you will find our robots from last year, and how we did in last years competition

A Quick Explanation of Botball

Botball is a completely autonomous robotics program. It requires the use of a multitude of sensors to have accurate robot driving. In the competition, you can have up to two robots on the game table, and each can run for up to 2 minutes.

Last Year's theme was packaging/construction. Therefore robots had to collect cones, poms, cubes(packages), drums, and more!

Some More Information Related to Last Years Game:

Last Year's game was a little special, in the sense that this was the year that we had 2 levels to the game board. There was an upper level, and a lower level. Robots could start on the upper level in the Upper Start Box (USB), or they could start on the lower level in the Lower Start Box (LSB). Additionally, to travel between the levels robots would have to navigate a ramp. The ramp did not have any smooth corners, causing for some issues with under-robot clearance. 

Drums are 1" PVC Pipes. They come out of the Automatic Drum Dispenser (ADDs).

The ADDs is a 3D printed dispensing machine for the drums. It releases the 1st drum 10 seconds after the game starts, and all other drums at a 7 second interval afterwards. Unfortunately, due to some bad design and the lack of nanometer precision from 3D printers, the drums would often come out at an angle instead of perfectly straight

Drum Posts are 1/2" PVC pipes that are directly attached to the game table. Drums can be placed on Drum Posts for points

Poms are placed on both the upper start box (USB), and lower start box (LSB). To score points with Poms you can either get a clean deck (clearing all poms out of the upper level), put Poms in trays for points, or put Poms in any game area. 

Botguy is KIPR's mascot, and therefore he is almost in every single game. Last year Botguy was in the center of the board guarded by two doors and cubes. To grab Botguy and score one would need to remove the cubes, open the doors, and then finally grab Botguy. One would also need to do this faster than their opponent as there is only one Botguy on the board. 

At the Competition:

The Competition is separated into 3 phases, practice, seeding, and Double Elimination.

Practice is around 3 days at GCER and 3-5 hours at Regionals. Practice is where robots are run to account for slight variations in different game tables. Practice happens because Botball is completely autonomous.

Seeding rounds are done to assess the scoring potential of each team without interference from other teams. 

Double Elimination is used to "pit" teams against each other to determine the highest scoring team.

Last Year's Competition

We, the founders, come from two teams, North County Robotics (NCR) and Hammered Screws. Therefore this sections will cover both of them.

Strategy:

The highest scoring strategy last year was to collect the drums and place them on PVC pipes. Therefore, North County Robotics, decided to have one robot, El Niño (yes that is the robots name) collect drums, and put them on the PVC pipes. On the other hand, Hammered Screws decided to have one robot, Nova, collect Botguy. The other robot for NCR and Hammered Screws were "safety robots". Safety Robots are robots that do easy tasks and are guaranteed to work 100% of the time. 

Awards:

Last year NCR won a Judges Choice Award at San Diego Regionals, a 3rd place Double Elimination award at Palm Desert Regionals, and a 1st place Double Elimination Award, 1st place overall award, and a "Do the Math, Save the World" award at GCER (Global Conference on Educational Robotics). 

Hammered Screws won 3rd place overall at San Diego Regionals, a 3rd place seeding at Palm Desert Regionals, a KISS (Keep it Simple Students) award at ECER (European Conference on Educational Robotics), and a Judge's choice for Great Programming at GCER.
